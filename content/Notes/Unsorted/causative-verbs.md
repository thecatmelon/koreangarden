## Basics
- **causative verb** = a verb that contains the meaning of causing something else to happen.
	- to eat = 먹다  vs "to let someone eat" (to feed) = 먹이다
	- to know = 알다  vs "to let someone know" (to tell) = 알리다
- Some *adjectives* have corresponding *causative verbs*:
	-  high (눞다)-> to increase (눞이다)
	- bright (밝다) -> to brighten/reveal (밝히다)
- To change verb/adjective to causative verb, you add one of the following after the stem:
	- 이, 히, 리, 기, 우, 구, 추

## When to use
- causative verbs in english (let, make, etc) usually used with another verb
- in Korean, causative verbs are used individually

### General verb vs causative verb.
- Compare these two sentences:
	- General verb: 딸은 밥을 먹었어요 (The daughter ate rice). 
	- Causative verb: 어머니가 딸에게 밥을 먹였어요 (The mother fed rice to the daughter).
- The results of both sentences have the daughter eating rice. 
	- But in sentence 1, the subject is the daughter. 
	- In the second sentence, *mother* is the subject.
	- make note of the particles
	
- Another example:
	- General verb: 모기가 죽었어요. (The mosquito died.)
	- Causative verb: 아빠가 모기를 죽였어요.(Dad killed the mosquito)
- In sentence 1, we have no idea how the mosquito died. could have died on its own or by weather, etc
- When causative verb used, the mosquito becomes the *object* (check the particles), and we know it was killed by *someone*.
